      # A clear line between illustration and evidence

      This site design uses illustrative images to communicate materials, mood and possible door styles. They must not be mistaken for photographs of work completed by Edina, proof of service coverage or endorsements from real customers.

      A compelling design is not a substitute for a real job record. Project pages remain clearly labelled drafts until Michael supplies actual work, facts and permission-cleared photography.

      ## What you are looking at
    items:
      - question: Are the doorway scenes completed Edina projects?
        answer: The illustrative door scenes used in this design are not presented as completed Edina work. The migrated site also includes product imagery and rendered scenes. A range image describes a style; it does not prove who manufactured, supplied or installed the pictured setting. Confirm an exact product using its range information and quote, not the image alone.
      - question: What are the close-up material images?
        answer: The editorial brass, handle and workshop studies are AI-generated illustrations. They are not close-up photographs of Edina workmanship, a named supplier’s finish samples, a real workshop or durability evidence. Colour, grain, joins and proportions are illustrative rather than specifications.
      - question: Is the portrait really Michael Dunn?
        answer: No. The portrait is a fictional AI-generated adult stand-in. The file name is an authoring label, not proof of identity. The [About page](/about/) retains Michael’s existing biography and visibly identifies the image as not Michael.
      - question: Is the family pictured Michael’s family?
        answer: No. The family scene uses fictional generated stand-ins. It does not show Michael, his wife or their two daughters. The biographical information is separate from the illustration.
      - question: Is the on-site image a real installation?
        answer: No. It is a staged fictional AI-generated demonstration, not Michael or a real customer job. It is not before-and-after evidence and does not verify work in Noosa or any other town.
      - question: Are the illustrative project briefs real case studies?
        answer: No. The Buderim, Maroochydore and Noosa briefs are fictional planning scenarios, not completed projects. They have visible draft notes and are excluded from search indexing. Their location labels do not establish [service coverage](/service-areas/).

      ## What real project evidence will require

      A real case study needs the actual project scope, Michael’s account of the decisions and customer-approved photographs of the work. Captions should make clear what is pictured and avoid revealing house numbers, faces, access codes or private details without permission.

      Illustrations should remain labelled if retained beside real photography. They must never be relabelled as an installation, a customer, a licence check or a testimonial.
